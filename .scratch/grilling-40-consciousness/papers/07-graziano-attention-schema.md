# Attention Schema Theory: Consciousness as an Attention Model

> Graziano, "Consciousness and the attention schema: Why it has to be right" — Cognitive Neuropsychology, 2020
> Wilterson, Kemper, Kim, Webb, Reblando & Graziano, "Attention control and the attention schema theory of consciousness" — Progress in Neurobiology 195, 2020
> Graziano, Guterstam, Bio & Wilterson, "Toward a standard model of consciousness" — Cognitive Neuropsychology, 2020

## Core Claim

Consciousness is NOT a mysterious inner essence. It is the brain's **internal model of its own attention** — an "attention schema" — analogous to the body schema (the brain's model of its own body). The brain constructs this simplified, schematic model of attention for CONTROL purposes (to better deploy attention), and because the model omits the mechanistic details of attention, it describes attention as an insubstantial, private essence — which is what we introspectively report as "consciousness."

## I. The Body Schema Analogy

### 1.1 What a Body Schema Does
- The brain constructs an internal model of the body's physical structure and state
- This model is simplified (schematized), not neurally detailed
- The model enables CONTROL — you can reach for an object without computing joint angles
- You are not aware of the body schema itself; you are aware OF your body THROUGH it

### 1.2 The Attention Schema
Exactly the same logic applied to attention:
- The brain constructs an internal model of its own attentional state
- This model describes attention in simplified, non-mechanistic terms
- It enables CONTROL of attention — better deployment, sustained focus, shifting
- **Consciousness IS the information content of this model** — not a separate phenomenon, but what the model represents

### 1.3 Why It "Feels Like Something"
The attention schema describes attention as:
- Having an **ineffable quality** (because mechanistic details are omitted)
- Being **private** (because it's an internal model, not accessible to others)
- Having **aboutness/intentionality** (because attention itself is about selecting information)

These properties of the MODEL are what we report as the properties of "conscious experience." There is no actual "magical amulet" of consciousness — there is a brain constructing a useful but incomplete self-model.

## II. Five Claims That Define the Theory

1. **The brain computes an attention schema**: a set of information that models the current state of attention (what is being attended, with what intensity, in what sensory modality)

2. **The attention schema is necessary for adequate attention control**: without a model of attention, the brain's ability to control attention is impaired — just as without a body schema, motor control is impaired

3. **The content of the attention schema is the basis for subjective reports of consciousness**: when we introspect and report awareness, we are accessing the attention schema's representation

4. **The attention schema is NOT the only factor**: social cognition (theory of mind), language, and memory also contribute to our CONCEPT of consciousness

5. **Consciousness attribution to others**: when we attribute consciousness to another person, we are using our own attention schema to model their attentional state

## III. AST vs. Other Theories

### 3.1 AST vs. Illusionism (Dennett, Frankish)
AST IS a form of illusionism — but with a specific computational mechanism. The "illusion" is not a mistake but a useful simplification: the brain correctly models attention while INCORRECTLY representing it as a non-physical essence. The error is in the model's incompleteness (omitting mechanistic detail), not in any explicit false claim.

### 3.2 AST vs. GNW
AST explains WHY global ignition and broadcasting FEEL LIKE consciousness: the attention schema monitors and controls which representations gain access to the global workspace. The workspace is the MECHANISM; the attention schema is the MODEL of that mechanism.

### 3.3 AST vs. IIT
AST agrees that integration is important — but it's integration specifically of ATTENTION-RELEVANT information, not arbitrary information. An expander graph has high Φ but no attention to model, therefore no consciousness.

### 3.4 AST vs. Higher-Order Theories
Both involve a "second layer" above first-order processing. But AST's second layer models ATTENTION specifically, not perceptual content in general. The meta-representation is about the attentional state, not about the first-order representation.

## IV. Empirical Evidence Cited

- **Attention-control experiments**: manipulating awareness (via masking) impairs attention control in ways predicted by AST (Wilterson et al., 2020)
- **Social attribution**: brain regions involved in modeling one's own attention overlap with regions used to model others' attention (TPJ, mPFC)
- **Hemineglect**: damage to attention-related areas also impairs awareness of the affected side — consistent with attention and awareness sharing underlying mechanisms

## V. Mathematical Status (for our question)

### What AST mathematically COULD define:
- **Attention state space**: a set of variables representing what is attended, with what intensity, in what modality
- **Schema function**: a mapping from neural attention states to simplified model-state representations
- **Control optimization**: how the attention schema improves attention deployment (formalizable as a control-theoretic problem)

### What AST currently LACKS mathematically:
- **A precise computational model**: as noted by Dolega & Dewhurst (2019), "the attention schema theory lacks a plausible computational formalization that could be used for developing possible mechanistic models"
- **Quantitative predictions**: the theory describes architecture and mechanism qualitatively but doesn't produce numerical predictions
- **A Φ-like scalar measure**: no single number captures "degree of consciousness" in AST

### Current State:
AST's mathematical development lags significantly behind IIT, FEP, and GNW — not because it's inherently unformalizable, but because its proponents have focused on conceptual and experimental development rather than mathematical formalism. Attempts to provide computational formalization (Dolega & Dewhurst 2019) use the predictive processing framework, suggesting AST may be absorbed into FEP/active inference as a specification of WHICH self-model matters for consciousness.
